# Code of conduct

This is a study tool for a course, and the people who show up here are mostly
students and the people who teach them. Treat the issue tracker, the pull
requests, and the discussions the way you would treat a lab section: assume
the other person is trying to learn or trying to help, and write as if they
will read it tomorrow morning.

## What is expected

- Be direct about the code and kind about the person. "This decodes the wrong
  bit" is a fine review comment; a remark about the author is not.
- Show your work. A bug report carries the program and the two outputs that
  disagree; a review comment carries the line and the reason.
- Keep course material where it belongs. Do not post assignment solutions,
  exam questions, or anything a course asks you not to share.
- Respect people's time. Search before opening a duplicate, and close what you
  opened once it is answered.

## What is not tolerated

Harassment, personal attacks, discrimination, sexual language or imagery,
publishing someone's private information, and sustained disruption of a
thread after being asked to stop. This applies in every space the project
runs: issues, pull requests, reviews, and any chat that grows around it.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainer through the repository's
security advisory form, which reaches only him:
<https://github.com/Abdalla-Eldoumani/aarch64-playground/security/advisories/new>.
Say what happened and where, and include links. Reports are read within a few
days, kept confidential, and answered.

## Enforcement

The maintainer decides what happens next, in proportion to what happened: a
private note, an edited or removed comment, a temporary block from the
repository, or a permanent one for repeated or severe behaviour. A decision
can be appealed once, through the same private channel, with anything the
first report did not have.
